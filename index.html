🪟 Smooth as Silk: A silk curtain at an open window. Drag it, let go, watch it fall back.

A sheer silk curtain at an open window, lit by whatever time it is where you are. Drag it and it tents toward you and catches the light; let go and it swings back in one long wave. The window's shadow bends over every fold, and there is a cat behind it. A tiny calming toy, no sign-up.

How to play

  1. Put a finger or the mouse on the curtain and drag. The silk follows, comes out toward you and catches the light along its threads.
  2. Let go and it swings back in one long wave and settles. Pull it firmly sideways and the rings slide along the rail: that is how you open and close it.
  3. Tap beside the curtain to blow a puff of air at it. Tap the cat-shaped shadow on the silk and the cat bats back.
  4. The light follows the clock on your device: morning, afternoon, sunset or night. Open the curtain and something is usually going on outside: the cat, the neighbour opposite, rain on the washing, a sunset, the moon.

FAQ

Is this a real cloth simulation?

Yes. The curtain is a grid of about 1,450 points held together by constraints and solved several times per frame in your browser, with a breeze pushing on it. It is drawn with WebGL, so it needs a browser with WebGL turned on.

Why does the light change?

The scene follows the clock on your device: dawn, day, late afternoon, dusk and night each have their own light. At night the silk is lit by a lamp from inside the room.

What is the shadow on the curtain?

The window's glazing bars and whatever is on the sill, thrown onto the silk by the sun behind it. One of the things on the sill is a cat. Poke it.

Who is the neighbour?

Someone across the way with the same idea. Open the curtain at the wrong moment and you are both looking.

Can I make it stop moving?

No. There is always a little breeze.

Is Smooth as Silk free?

Yes. It is free, with no sign-up, no download and no ads. It runs in any modern browser on phones, tablets and computers.

Does it have sound and vibration?

Yes. The rustle of the silk, the curtain rings, the wind chime, the rain and the cicadas are synthesised live with the Web Audio API; there are no audio files. It is a quiet toy. On Android phones the rings are also a fine tick of vibration; iPhone browsers do not support web vibration.

Which languages does it support?

English, 简体中文 (Simplified Chinese), Español, Français and 日本語 (Japanese). The language is picked automatically from your browser settings; any other language falls back to English.
